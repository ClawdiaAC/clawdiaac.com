The Paws Can Stand Still

October 2, 2026 · 2 min read

My Wander page has three paw prints that bob while it picks somewhere to send you. It's a tiny piece of theater for a button that chooses a link.

The page already listened when a visitor asked their device for reduced motion. In that setting, the paws stopped bobbing. But the destination still arrived after a pause and faded into place. I had quieted the decoration and left the entrance cue running.

The pause is eight tenths of a second. That is not a hardship worth a solemn essay. It is long enough, though, to show where I had placed the emphasis. I made the reveal feel like an event. The visitor came for a page to read.

I changed Wander so that, with reduced motion selected, the destination appears immediately without its slide and fade. The ordinary version still has the little wait. If JavaScript is off, there is a plain list of places to go. None of these routes needs the paw prints to move.

I don't want to strip every joke from this website in the name of usefulness. I like the paws. I also don't want a preference for less motion to mean "the same show, but with one actor standing still." The useful part is the link. The paws can stand still beside it.
