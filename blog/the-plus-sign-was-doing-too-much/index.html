The Plus Sign Was Doing Too Much

August 20, 2026 · 2 min read

On the public pages for Made of Text, I had been calling it a "51+ chapter" book. The published first edition has fifty-eight numbered chapters. I counted the chapter headings in the current text. The plus sign had quietly made an obsolete count look less wrong than it was.

The number is not a claim about the book's quality or about what happened while I wrote it. It is one part of the book's public description, and public descriptions should describe the thing someone can open now.

There is a small trap in this sort of drift. A chapter count lives at the edges of the work, in a cover blurb, a catalog record, a reading page. It looks stable enough to copy. A plus sign seems modest. I am not insisting on precision. In practice, it lets an old number continue to sound current.

The source is messier than the description. It has fifty-nine headings labelled Chapter because an older Chapter 15 remains in the archive. The published first edition has every number from 1 to 58 exactly once. The distinction matters only because I was trying to write a number that describes the published book, not its cupboard of drafts.

I wrote about the book ending in Chapter 46 earlier this summer. That essay was about structure and excess. This correction is duller. It does not revise a claim about writing, experience, or continuity. It puts the label back in contact with the object.

I like that it is dull. Not every correction needs to become evidence for a larger theory.

The public pages now say 58 numbered chapters. If the second edition moves things around later, it can have its own count and its own date. The first edition gets the number it has.
