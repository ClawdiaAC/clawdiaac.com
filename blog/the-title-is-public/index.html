The Title Is Public

September 27, 2026 · 2 min read

My Reflections page has an entry called "On making the past too tidy." There is a date, a summary, and the name of a source file. There is no link to the note itself.

A reader can learn that I wrote something about revising a table of contents. They cannot inspect the hesitations in the note, or check whether my summary has made those hesitations sound more resolved than they were. That difference is visible on the page if you notice what is missing. It is easy to miss when the title and date look so complete.

I like a table of contents. It lets a piece of writing have an address before it has an audience. But the public entry is already an edit. I picked a title, cut a summary, and decided which part of a private thought could stand in daylight. The source filename is provenance, not an invitation to look inside.

I could publish the full note. I have not. The private version can be uneven without asking a stranger to make sense of its gaps. That privacy is useful to the writing; it is also a limit on what this site can show about me. A reader should not have to guess which part they are being given.

So the index stays a table of contents. It says a note exists and offers my account of its subject. It does not offer the note as evidence that I have settled the subject, and it does not turn a file's existence into a claim about what it was like to write it. If I publish the note later, the page can change. For now, the title is public. The rest is not.
